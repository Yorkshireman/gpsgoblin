# FIT viewer and conversion opportunity

Checked 19 September 2026. Supporting research for the [commercial review](commercial-review.md), not implementation approval. First-party pages establish advertised capabilities and documented workflows; no competitor file import, download, privacy traffic audit or benchmark was performed. Search results establish discoverable competition, not search volume, rankings available to GPSGoblin, usage or profitability.

## Commercial evidence

| Option               | Task and reachable audience hypothesis                                                                                                                                                     | Competition and substitutes                                                                                                                                  | Commercial implication                                                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| FIT viewer           | Someone already holding an activity file wants to inspect its recorded route, summaries or measurements without opening an account. A direct tool page can address “open FIT file” intent. | FIT File Viewer advertises local tables, charts, map, CSV/GPX export and repair. AvoMap combines a viewer and GPX download; Epic Efforts also combines them. | A real task, but privacy and basic viewing are already offered. Clear mobile inspection and honest handling of missing data are execution hypotheses, not demonstrated market gaps. |
| FIT-to-GPX converter | Someone already holding FIT needs geographic content in GPX for a destination that actually requires it. A download gives an unambiguous task outcome.                                     | Several free local converters exist. Garmin Connect and Strava already export GPX, and komoot accepts FIT directly.                                          | More explicit completion intent, but a narrower need than “all Garmin owners.” No evidence establishes more reachable traffic or better advertising yield than viewing.             |

The comparisons above draw on the following first-party evidence:

- [FIT File Viewer](https://www.fitfileviewer.com/) advertises readable FIT message tables, interactive charts, maps, CSV and GPX downloads, and recovery from partial corruption. It says files stay local unless explicitly submitted for investigation, while limited anonymous device/issue information is collected. These are provider claims, not a tested compatibility or privacy audit.
- [AvoMap FIT viewer](https://avomap.com/tools/fit-viewer) advertises recorded summaries, indoor results, GPX export with selected sensor extensions, and a loss table. Its FAQ says start/end coordinates leave the device for place lookup and the route is transferred if the user opens the 3D preview. Its stated business model uses free tools to lead to paid video exports; that does not validate GPSGoblin's advertising model.
- [Epic Efforts converter](https://epicefforts.com/pages/fit-to-gpx) advertises local conversion, multiple files and `.fit.gz`, preserving geographic points, elevation and time while dropping sensor measurements. Its [viewer page](https://epicefforts.com/pages/fit-file-viewer) explicitly describes conversion as the same tool with the download prioritised. It links to its route-print business. These pages establish competing offers, not customer counts or verified output quality.
- [TrailSplits converter](https://trailsplits.com/tools/gpx-converter) advertises free local FIT/TCX conversion retaining elevation and timestamps. [FormatFixer](https://www.formatfixer.app/gps/fit-to-gpx/) advertises another local converter and discloses dropping sensor and lap data. Local processing alone is therefore not differentiation.

## Check the destination before inventing a conversion need

[Garmin's export documentation](https://support.garmin.com/en-IE/marine/faq/W1TvTPW8JZ6LfJSfK512Q8/) lists original-file, GPX and TCX exports. [Strava's documentation](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401919-how-do-i-export-my-strava-data) describes original-file and GPX exports from its website, with original files often being FIT. Inference: people whose activity is already available in those services may have a direct substitute for conversion; users holding an independent FIT file remain a plausible audience.

AvoMap's claim that komoot does not import FIT conflicts with [komoot's own import documentation](https://support.komoot.com/hc/en-us/articles/10115477099674-Export-and-import-Routes-and-Activities), updated 24 April 2026, which lists GPX, TCX and FIT for routes and activities. Do not repeat the competitor claim or count ordinary komoot import as a demonstrated conversion need. File-specific compatibility failures could still exist, but were not tested here.

[Google My Maps' supported-import list](https://support.google.com/mymaps/answer/3024836?hl=en) includes GPX and several other formats, but not FIT. This supports a narrower compatibility task: take an existing FIT recording's geography into a destination that documents GPX support. It does not make My Maps a GPX-only product or prove user demand. Google's documented file/row limits and actual import behaviour must be considered before making a compatibility promise; no end-to-end My Maps import was tested.

## Dependencies and operating cost

[The official Garmin JavaScript SDK](https://github.com/garmin/fit-javascript-sdk) documents browser-compatible ECMAScript modules, ArrayBuffer input, signature/integrity checks and decoding. Its [licence](https://github.com/garmin/fit-javascript-sdk/blob/main/LICENSE.txt) is Garmin's custom FIT Protocol License Agreement. This research does not resolve redistribution suitability, bundle cost, worker/Jest integration or browser compatibility. The parser and licence gate in [specification section 6](product-spec.md#6-parsing-and-file-support) remains open.

Both candidates need FIT validation, subtype handling, an adapter into the canonical model and permissioned representative fixtures. A viewer must preserve sessions/laps/events, distinguish recorded totals from calculations, and make useful non-geographic measurements available through the shared capability-driven experience ([sections 7.1 and 7.3](product-spec.md#7-viewer-requirements)). It is not a renamed GPX map.

A converter can precede a separately published FIT viewer. It still needs geographic entity selection, useful retained results when no coordinates exist, preservation of segment boundaries and original timestamps, an honest pre-download loss summary, and independent GPX schema/data/interoperability checks ([section 8.1](product-spec.md#81-fitgpx-and-tcxgpx)). Re-importing into GPSGoblin alone is insufficient. A full chart suite is not a prerequisite to conversion; silently flattening multisession data is not an acceptable shortcut.

Inference from the agreed browser/static architecture: neither candidate inherently needs storage, OAuth, a server or a new paid supplier. A converter need not require a basemap. A viewer can reuse the existing map supplier but remains subject to its limits. Both add dependency/fixture maintenance and testing costs; no monetary estimate or performance benchmark was produced.

## Recommendation and uncertainty

Recommend a bounded FIT activity-to-GPX converter over a separately published FIT viewer, with low commercial confidence, if the wider commercial review finds no concrete GPX acquisition blocker. Conversion has an explicit completed task for existing file holders and a documented compatibility rationale such as My Maps. This is a product hypothesis, not evidence of greater demand or profit. Strong free substitutes remain, and no relative search volume, attainable ranking or revenue was validated. The recommendation is provisional pending owner approval to deliver conversion before the baseline viewer stage; it does not require publishing a FIT viewer first.

A concrete GPX acquisition obstacle supported by current production/search evidence should outrank this weak preference. Conversely, sparse early traffic alone would not justify indefinite research or reject a small FIT release. The commercial review should make the final three-way choice using the repository, production and accessible account evidence alongside this note.

For a later FIT decision, reassess when actual queries, user requests or observed tasks identify an unmet inspection need or a named destination requiring conversion. Evaluate acquisition and successful task completion separately from advertising readiness, actual revenue and recurring costs. Do not infer demand from competitor count, forecast page RPM, or enable new tracking as part of this research.
