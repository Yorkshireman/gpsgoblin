# Commercial review

**Last reviewed:** 20 September 2026. **Status:** [ticket #55](https://github.com/Yorkshireman/gpsgoblin/issues/55) remains open, but its decoder dependency gate is not currently satisfied. Garmin's reply is pending and no reviewed third-party decoder has both acceptable provenance and the required correctness. No commercial performance dataset was accessible.

The [product spec](product-spec.md), especially sections 2, 3.3 and 23, owns strategy. This record follows the [commercial review workflow](../AGENTS.md#commercial-project-management-what-next). Recommendations do not authorise implementation, publishing, spending, telemetry or merging.

## Current recommendation

**Next:** do not begin the public FIT converter while its decoder gate is unresolved. The recommended default is to defer #55 and assess TCX-to-GPX or another commercially evidenced task. If the owner still prioritises FIT before Garmin replies, the only worthwhile FIT work is a separately approved, time-boxed narrow-decoder feasibility spike with strict provenance and correctness stop gates; it must stop before UI implementation if either gate fails.

**Why now:** exact-artifact review found that `fit-file-parser@5.2.1` mechanically republishes a complete profile generated from Garmin's restrictively licensed SDK, so its MIT declaration is not a defensible permission chain for those tables. The wider candidate review found the same SDK/profile provenance in the other plausible browser libraries or correctness, maintenance and integration failures that make them unsuitable. Continuing package searches is unlikely to change the decision.

**Evidence and uncertainty:** a deliberately narrow decoder is technically plausible because FIT files carry their binary field layouts, but useful conversion still needs semantic mappings for subtype, sessions, laps, events, coordinates, timestamps and elevation. Those mappings need an independently defensible source and representative-device validation. See the [exact package audit](fit-file-parser-license-research.md) and [remaining decoder review](fit-decoder-options-research.md). This is not a conclusion that independent FIT implementations are unlawful; it is a conclusion that the reviewed evidence is insufficient for GPSGoblin's release gate. FIT acquisition and profit confidence remains low, and no evidence shows TCX would attract more demand.

**Why not the alternatives:** another full-profile package merely repeats the provenance problem. GPSBabel is a maintained GPL C++/Qt application rather than a practical browser dependency; adapting it would add substantial integration and licensing work without directly satisfying session preservation. A backend or third-party conversion service would violate the selected local-file architecture and introduce privacy, operating-cost and supplier questions. TCX is only a candidate: it needs its own schema/licence, demand and preservation review before selection.

**Done means:** either the owner explicitly approves a narrow FIT spike and it produces a technically correct decoder with a field-by-field defensible provenance record, or #55 is deferred and a bounded alternative is selected through the normal commercial review. A spike is not a released converter or evidence of demand. Commercial success remains useful visits that contribute revenue above operating costs; no profit uplift is forecast.

## Monetisation and assessment plan

Google's [AdSense eligibility guidance](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9724?hl=en) and [site-readiness guidance](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/7299563?hl=en) emphasise original useful content, audience and usable navigation. They do not establish a required tool count or guarantee approval. [Google's consent requirements](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/13554020?hl=en) must be assessed before advertising in affected regions. This investigation neither applies for AdSense nor claims legal compliance. Advertising readiness should be revisited at the next release checkpoint, rather than deferred until the entire suite exists.

For the selected investment, record launch date, tested scope and known defects. Use existing Search Console access if available to record a pre-release baseline and then review indexing, queries, impressions and clicks for the converter and GPX page after approximately **four weeks of public availability**, with a subsequent review around eight weeks if observations are sparse. These are review windows, not automatic failure thresholds or deadlines. [Search Console's performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553?hl=en) supplies search metrics, not successful conversions. If access stays unavailable, label acquisition impact unknown and use permissioned task observations and reported interoperability outcomes; do not require new tracking.

Assess successful downloading/opening separately from acquisition, using representative synthetic/permissioned files and, when available, voluntary user feedback. When ads are separately approved and active, review actual revenue and domain/hosting/map/other costs over matching periods. Record maintenance effort separately. Reassess earlier for correctness failures, confirmed indexing blockers, decoder/licence incompatibility, significant supplier changes or clear evidence favouring another task. Do not expand formats, batch conversion or FIT analysis solely because this release is technically complete.
